Research Interests

How do we design production systems when multiple stakeholders are involved? My research builds from classical process optimization techniques and incorporates game-theoretical and reinforcement learning frameworks for multi-agent integration.

01

Foundations

Process Design Fundamentals and Optimization

Process design turns a minimal processing pathway into a working industrial-grade production system. Determining network topology, operating conditions, equipment sizing, and consolidating them into a flowsheet has for decades relied on engineering heuristics, conceptual design and optimization approaches.

Mathematical programming and rigorous process simulation let us move from rules of thumb to a systematic search over large design spaces, including superstructures, capacity expansion and multi-period planning. I used these tools to design green hydrogen production systems and plan their capacity expansion, balancing economic and operational objectives.

02

Modern tools

Multi-agent process system networks

Most design methods assume a single decision-maker. Real production networks are different: a hydrogen producer, a methanol plant and a fuel refinery may share land, utilities and pipelines, yet each one has its own economics and goals. My doctoral research develops game-theoretical and reinforcement learning frameworks to design these multi-agent systems, so that the resulting designs are efficient as a whole and acceptable to every participant.

03

Current focus

Autonomous and machine-aided industrial management

In multi-agent systems, stakeholders and firms must coordinate their efforts in order to succesfully design, deploy and operate interconnected productive systems. In practice, industrial collaboration is a human-guided process with a diverse amount of challenges, such as time constraints, slow-paced agreement settling procedures, limited capacity for design space awareness and privacy concerns.
